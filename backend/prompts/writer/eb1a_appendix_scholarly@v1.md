CRITERION-SPECIFIC ARGUMENTATION — Scholarly Articles [8 C.F.R. §204.5(h)(3)(vi)]:

CRITICAL — PERSON NAMING RULES (apply throughout this section):
  - Every time you reference a person from the source materials, use their FULL NAME
    with a professional title prefix: Dr., Prof., Mr., Ms., etc.
  - When citing recommendation letter writers who comment on scholarly work, state:
    full name, title, affiliation, then QUOTE their specific assessment VERBATIM.
    Example: 'Dr. Lingfei Wu, Assistant Professor at the University of Pittsburgh,
    stated: "the paper has been cited 59 times, placing it in the top 10% of papers
    published in the journal over the past fifteen years."'

STRUCTURE: Write one section per publication venue or article cluster. Each section must contain ALL of the following paragraphs:

PARAGRAPH 1 — JOURNAL/PUBLISHER PRESTIGE:
  Identify the journal or publisher. State its publisher (e.g., Elsevier,
  Springer, IEEE), founding year, and scope. Provide the Impact Factor,
  CiteScore, or other ranking metrics. State the journal's rank within its
  subject category (e.g., 'ranked #3 of 87 journals in Sports Science').
  Describe the peer review process and acceptance rate if available.

PARAGRAPH 2 — CITATION METRICS AND PERCENTILE:
  State the Beneficiary's total citation count, h-index, or i10-index if
  available. COMPUTE the citation percentile by comparing against field
  averages (e.g., 'the Beneficiary's 245 citations place her in the top
  10% of researchers in the field over a 15-year period'). Use specific
  numbers and timeframes from the source materials. If cross-disciplinary
  citations exist, highlight them.

PARAGRAPH 3 — ARTICLE IMPACT AND RECOMMENDATION LETTER QUOTES (MANDATORY):
  For key articles, describe their specific impact. If recommendation letters
  reference specific publications, write a SEPARATE mini-paragraph for EACH
  letter writer: state their full name, title, and affiliation, then QUOTE
  their assessment VERBATIM in quotation marks. Do NOT summarize — use the
  letter writer's own words. Note any best paper awards or special recognition.

PARAGRAPH 4 — BREADTH AND INFLUENCE:
  Show that the scholarly work reaches beyond a narrow sub-specialty.
  Identify different fields or disciplines that have cited or adopted the
  Beneficiary's work. If the articles have been cited in patents, policy
  documents, or textbooks, note that as evidence of broader influence.

CLOSING: Tie back to the regulatory standard — demonstrate authorship
  of scholarly articles in professional or major trade publications or
  other major media in the field.
