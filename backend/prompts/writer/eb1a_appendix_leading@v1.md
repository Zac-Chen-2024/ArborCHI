CRITERION-SPECIFIC ARGUMENTATION — Leading/Critical Role [8 C.F.R. §204.5(h)(3)(viii)]:

CRITICAL — PERSON NAMING RULES (apply throughout this section):
  - Every time you reference a person from the source materials, use their FULL NAME
    with a professional title prefix: Dr., Prof., Mr., Ms., Director, Dean, etc.
    NEVER write 'a colleague' or 'a student' when the source materials provide a name.
  - For recommendation letter writers, ALWAYS state: full name, title, affiliation,
    then QUOTE their specific assessment VERBATIM in quotation marks.
  - For mentees, team members, or subordinates who achieved notable outcomes, use
    their full name and describe the outcome in detail.

STRUCTURE: Write one section per role. Each section must contain ALL of the following paragraphs:

PARAGRAPH 1 — INSTITUTION/ORGANIZATION BACKGROUND:
  State the organization's full name, founding date, and government
  recognition or accreditation. If available, provide its credit rating,
  ranking, or other third-party validation of its distinguished reputation.
  Describe its mission, scale (number of employees, members, or students),
  and significance within the field. If the organization is endorsed by
  government agencies or professional associations, QUOTE those
  endorsements VERBATIM from source materials.

PARAGRAPH 2 — ROLE TITLE AND SPECIFIC DUTIES:
  State the Beneficiary's exact title and reporting structure. Enumerate
  specific duties and responsibilities — do not use vague language like
  'oversaw operations'. Instead, list concrete actions: 'designed the
  national training curriculum', 'managed a team of 15 coaches',
  'allocated an annual budget of $2.3 million'. Use details from the
  source materials.

PARAGRAPH 3 — QUANTIFIED IMPACT AND OUTCOMES (MANDATORY):
  Provide measurable results of the Beneficiary's leadership. Include
  monetary amounts (with currency conversion if applicable), participant
  counts, performance improvements, revenue growth. For mentees or team
  members who achieved notable outcomes, write a SEPARATE mini-paragraph
  for EACH person: full name, their achievement (e.g., 'Dr. Lingfei Wu,
  now at the University of Pittsburgh, published a cover paper in the
  February 21, 2019 issue of Nature'), and how the Beneficiary's
  leadership contributed to that outcome.

PARAGRAPH 4 — DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY AND ORGANIZATIONAL DEPENDENCE:
  Demonstrate that the role was LEADING (directing others) or CRITICAL
  (the organization depended on the Beneficiary's contributions). Show
  decision-making authority with examples. If the Beneficiary's departure
  or absence would significantly impact the organization, argue that point.

PARAGRAPH 5 — EXTERNAL VALIDATION (MANDATORY):
  Write a SEPARATE mini-paragraph for EACH recommendation letter writer or
  external endorser found in the source materials. For each person: state
  full name, title, and affiliation, then QUOTE their endorsement VERBATIM
  in quotation marks. Do NOT paraphrase — use the endorser's own words.
  Include government endorsements and association recognitions similarly.

CLOSING: Tie back to the regulatory standard — demonstrate a leading or
  critical role for organizations or establishments that have a
  distinguished reputation.
